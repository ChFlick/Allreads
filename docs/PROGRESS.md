## Vocabulary check (branch `feature/vocab-check`, plan: IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN "Vocabulary check")

- [x] 1. Core: building and scoring the test (2026-11-27: `vocab_check::VocabCheck`, a session the app asks one band at a time: 6 dictionary forms per frequency band, 10 made-up words spliced from real ones and checked against the dictionary, a stop after two mostly-unknown bands, then 12 words around the reader's boundary. Scoring: corrected for false "yes", a falling fitted curve, and two choices with the accuracy they expect, "safe" (chance of knowing at least 95 %) and "more" (85 %). Words answered "no" are never marked; a "yes" beyond the cut-off only when at least 90 % likely real. Simulated readers knowing 300 to 15,000 words, honest or over-claiming 10 %: the safe choice is 98–99 % right in 41–88 questions, and the accuracy it shows is within a point of the real one. Tuned first on Lexique 3.83's real ranks in Python)
- [x] 2. dictgen: frequency sources into the dictionary (2026-11-27: `dictgen::frequency`, a `FrequencySource` trait with `Lexique` (forms with their dictionary forms, books and films averaged) and `FrequencyWords` (forms only; dictionary forms from the dictionary's own "form of" links). `add_frequencies` writes the same optional tables whatever the source (`frequency`: dictionary forms by rank; `frequency_form`: their forms; the source's name in `meta`), keeping only words the dictionary knows. `dictgen --frequencies FILE --frequency-source lexique|frequencywords`, also on an existing dictionary. The core reads them with `Dictionary::ranked_words`, `ranked_forms` and `frequency_source`. On the real Lexique 3.83 and the published French dictionary: 37,860 ranked dictionary forms with 125,374 forms. The Dictionaries workflow downloads Lexique for French and FrequencyWords for other languages, and keeps the published book lists in the manifest. Credits for both in About. The check no longer asks single letters or elided bits like "l'un")
- [x] 3. Core and FFI: marking and undo (2026-11-27: `VocabCheck::forms_to_mark` widens the marked dictionary forms to all their forms; `Database::mark_known_from_check` saves them as known with the source "Vocabulary check" and never touches words already saved; `undo_vocabulary_check` deletes the marked words still known with no meaning, notes or Anki card since. FFI: a `VocabCheckSession` object the app asks one band at a time (`next_words`, `answer`, `result`), and `vocabulary_check_available`, `start_vocabulary_check`, `apply_vocabulary_check`, `undo_vocabulary_check`, `vocabulary_check_count`. Starting a check takes about 2 ms, the result under 1 ms, applying it about 6 ms)

- [x] Accuracy review (2026-11-27): checked against the real French data. Cognates (about 40 % of words from rank 500 on look like their English translation) do not hurt: readers recognising 80 % or 95 % of them at any rank still get 98 % of the safe choice right, and fitting cognates on a separate curve was rejected (2–3 times the marks at about 95 % right). Made-up words improved: about half of the old ones were plainly fake ("profooire", "balusppe") and some close to real words ("amélette"); they now use only letter sequences common in real words and are never one letter from a common word ("troussure", "graloter", "sertance"). When too many made-up words get a "yes", nothing is offered and the result asks for a retake. With 25 % guessing the safe choice stays 97–98 % right.
