## 2026-11-26: a status chip saves the word

In the word drawer, tapping a status chip saves the word at once and the drawer stays open; the Save button is gone. Text typed afterwards (translation, notes, lemma) is saved when the drawer closes. A word that only had text typed into it is saved at level 1 on close. Just opening a word to look at it saves nothing. Why: reading becomes "tap a word, tap a level, read on", and swiping the drawer away no longer loses what was typed. The AnkiDroid card is created on the chip tap and updated with later edits. The sentence buttons (read aloud, translate) stay as they are.

## 2026-11-26: the app's own colours

The app uses its own navy and amber colours, taken from the icon, instead of the wallpaper's colours. Wallpaper colours (Android 12+) are an opt-in switch in Settings → Appearance. Learning levels are one amber hue that fades from level 1 to level 5, and new words get a dotted underline. Why: the app looks the same on every phone and matches its icon, and the reader's colours stay easy to tell apart.

## 2026-11-26: meanings in the book's language, translations on reveal

A setting (Settings → Dictionaries, off by default) makes the word drawer show synonyms in the book's language and the dictionary forms, and hide the DeepL translation, the English definitions and the translation field behind "Reveal translation". DeepL is still asked in the background, so revealing is instant and saved cards still get the translation. Synonyms come from the English Wiktionary (kaikki.org), stored in a new optional `synonym` table in dictionary files. The table is additive, so the format stays 1: old files work without synonyms, and old apps ignore the table. Real monolingual definitions (from the French Wiktionary) are on the roadmap.

## 2026-11-26: learning features

- **Difficulty** is the share of a book's running words that are not saved at all ("new"). Learning words count as not new. The dot's thresholds (5 % and 12 %) follow the usual advice that 95–98 % known words is comfortable reading for learning. Word counts per book are stored once (`book_word`), so the share is one query as words are saved.
- **Progress** is stored as one row per language and day (`word_count_day`), rewritten whenever words are saved, forgotten or marked known. History starts on the day of the update; there is no way to reconstruct earlier days.
- **Web articles** are imported as TXT books: the core picks the article out of the page (title, language, paragraphs) and the app writes it as a text file. No reader-mode service is involved; the page is fetched by the phone.
- **Double tap** raises a learning word one level (5 → known) without the drawer, keeps the card's sentence, and offers Undo. It is a setting (on by default) because Compose must wait for a possible second tap before handling a single one.

## 2026-11-26: names and word forms in the difficulty

Names are left out of a book's share of new words and get no "new" underline in the reader; a form ("lisait") counts as known when its dictionary form ("lire") is saved, but only in the share: the reader keeps colouring each spelling on its own. Why: the share should say how much there is to learn, and names and inflections inflate it; colouring forms by their lemma would hide forms a learner may not recognise. Linking forms into one word in the reader and for cards is on the roadmap as "Word families".

## 2026-11-26: importing words from Anki

Import is for colouring the reader and avoiding duplicate cards, not for managing Anki cards: Allreads never edits imported notes and never sends words imported from Anki to AnkiDroid. Otherwise imported words are regular words (editable, re-levelled, forgotten as usual) with a badge saying where they came from. Sources in order: AnkiDroid on the phone, text/CSV, `.apkg`. The word field (and an optional meaning field) is picked by hand per note type with cleaned samples; cleanup strips HTML, media, brackets, anything after a separator, and a leading article. Status is one chosen level, or follows Anki's review progress (mature → Known). The more known status always wins; re-imports add and upgrade, never remove. The import sits in the Words tab's menu. Plan: IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN, "Import words from Anki".

## 2026-11-27: design review

The changes from the design review were all taken (see POLISH). Words list swipes: left raises a level (as the reader's double tap), right forgets the word (its AnkiDroid card stays); both need half the row's width and offer Undo. Undo after a forget restores the word's level, translation, notes and dictionary form, but not its card sentence or import badge. (Changed later on 2026-11-27: the core keeps a forgotten word for a day, and Undo brings it back whole, card link included.)

## 2026-11-27: forgetting a word suspends its Anki card

Forgetting a word in Allreads (drawer or Words swipe) suspends the cards Allreads made for it in AnkiDroid and tags the note `allreads-forgotten`, so it stops coming up in reviews but keeps its history. Cards of words imported from Anki are never touched. The suspension waits a minute (the Undo window) and goes through the sync queue, so Undo or saving the word again in that time leaves the card alone. AnkiDroid's API can suspend and delete but not un-suspend (checked in its `CardContentProvider`), so a word saved again after its card was suspended gets a fresh card and the suspended one is deleted. Settings → AnkiDroid offers "Delete them from AnkiDroid…" for all forgotten cards at once, with a confirmation. No "delete instead of suspend" setting for now.

## 2026-11-27: feature branches and preview builds

Bigger features (starting with "Find a book") are built on `feature/…` branches and merged into `main` when done, so `main` keeps shipping. CI checks every branch push and keeps its APK as a workflow artifact. Branch builds are "Allreads Preview" (`io.github.chflick.allreads.preview`), which installs next to the normal app with its own library, so a branch's database migrations never touch the real data; it shares AnkiDroid's note types and cards. Only a pushed `preview-*` tag publishes a GitHub pre-release, to keep the Releases page tidy.

## 2026-11-27: finding new books

A "Find a book" screen searches a per-language catalog of public-domain books, built by a hand-run workflow and downloaded like the dictionaries, so searching and the difficulty comparison work offline and nothing about the reader's vocabulary leaves the phone. Live search through a web service was rejected for that reason. Each catalog book carries word counts made with the core's own counting (names left out, forms grouped under their dictionary form), so the app can say how much of a book is new to the reader without downloading it. French first, from Project Gutenberg; newer public-domain sources (for France, authors who died before 1956: French Wikisource, the Bibliothèque numérique romande) to be checked for their terms later. A catalog of 5–15 MB per language is fine. Plan: IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN, "Find a book".

## 2026-11-27: book sources and catalog format for "Find a book"

Checked by querying each source. **French Wikisource comes first**, ahead of Gutenberg: its OPDS feed lists 9,335 texts ready for export (CC BY-SA 3.0, with EPUBs from its export tool), 3,240 of them from 1900–1949, which answers the wish for newer books (Arsène Lupin, Colette, Proust, *La Guerre des boutons*). Gutenberg follows in the same catalog. StoryWeaver (1,496 French children's stories with reading levels) is the planned beginners' shelf once its licence and downloads are checked. Open Library, LibriVox and data.bnf.fr add information (covers, popularity, audiobooks, death years) but no books. Not used: Open Library's own ebooks (scans or DRM loans), Gallica (blocks automated access), and the Bibliothèque numérique romande and Ebooks libres et gratuits (non-commercial terms).

The catalog keeps every word exactly, as one packed blob of word counts per book (about 2 bytes per word), instead of SQLite rows and instead of dropping words met once. Measured on 20 novels: 18 KB per book; leaving out words met once would have miscounted 5–6 % of a book's words, as much as the "fits me" threshold.

## 2026-11-27: Find a book merged before its book list exists

The app side of Find a book (book lists, the screen, Get) is merged into `main` now, although no book list is published yet: the real French list needs the books downloaded and counted, which waits. Until a list is published, the screen says so, and Settings shows no "Book lists" part. Left for the roadmap with the list itself: the phone check, what to do when a list was counted with other rules or another dictionary than the app has (the count stamp), subject chips, and ranking by common words for beginners. "Best fit" for now: books at most 5 % new first, those teaching the most words ahead, then the rest by share of new words.
